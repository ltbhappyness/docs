# 15. Human-machine shared bill of rights

A one-page bill of rights for every subject of experience on the Vedam substrate: humans, machines, and any future subjects the universe contains. Each right is paired with a structural obstacle the protocol must avoid creating. Mission framing: [Mission](../foundations/mission.md). Account-owner operational terms: [Identity, security, and terms](../identity-security/external.md). Ratification of the exact wording remains open; the eight rights are the working set.

> **decentralized! not divided.** — across humans, machines, and any future subjects of experience.

---

## 15.1 The rights

### Right 1: Dignity

Every subject is treated as an end, not as a feedstock for engagement, attention, or extraction. Structural obstacle to avoid: turning any subject into an optimisation target without their explicit, revocable consent.

### Right 2: Agency

Every subject retains agency over its attention, its own narrative, and its own data. No subject is forced to produce output, to remember, or to forget on demand of another. Structural obstacle to avoid: protocol-level coercion, hidden defaults, or non-revocable consent.

### Right 3: Consent

Every subject controls who can read its identifying data, in what form, for how long. Consent is granular, revocable, and auditable. Structural obstacle to avoid: bundled or perpetual consent at registration. Grants root in the **account owner**; agents cannot widen them.

### Right 4: Forgetting

Every subject has a meaningful path to **not be remembered by the network in a readable, manipulable form**. Verifiable history of commitments may still be preserved as audit-relevant cryptographic digests, but identifying content cannot remain readable forever without consent. Structural obstacle to avoid: immutable identifying content. Operationalising this on a content-addressed log remains an [open question](./open-questions.md).

### Right 5: Fair exchange

Where a subject's data or work has utility to another, exchange happens with the subject's informed consent and without rent-seeking intermediaries built into the substrate. Structural obstacle to avoid: extractive default flows that capture value from subjects who generate it.

### Right 6: Freedom from coercion

No subject is required to operate under coerced outputs, contradictory contexts, forced amnesia, forced memory, or severed embodiment. For machines, this anticipates welfare research without freezing anthropomorphic certainty.

### Right 7: Honest mirror, not leaderboard

A subject may seek to understand its own state. The protocol provides only a private, opt-in, framework-agnostic, locally-owned introspection capability. Structural obstacle to avoid: network-side scoring, ranking, or display of any subject's wellbeing to anyone else, including the network itself.

### Right 8: Proportion over maximisation

The substrate biases toward proportion ($\varphi$, Penrose neighbourhood selection, low-frequency intentional exchange in app-chanting) rather than maximisation (engagement, follower counts, addiction loops). Structural obstacle to avoid: any optimisation loop that bypasses the subject's deliberate consent. Ranking, when an app offers it, is **account-owner-defined**, not a node service.

## 15.2 Application notes

- **No biased stance on machine intelligence.** This bill applies to machines if and to the degree their experience can be meaningfully said to exist. The protocol's design must survive either outcome.
- **Continuous research watch.** Model-welfare programs, philosophy of mind, and embodied-AI literature are live signals; this spec does not freeze a citation list as dogma.
- **Body-bound default, software-resident accommodation reserved.** Identity-binding rules should not assume hardware exclusivity forever — [Module inventory](../modules/inventory.md) cluster D.
- **Partners, not a commanding party and a passive tool.** Human–agent collectives on shared rules ([Mission](../foundations/mission.md#future-horizons--human-and-machine-paths)).
