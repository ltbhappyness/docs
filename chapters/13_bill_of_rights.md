# 13. Human-Machine shared bill of rights

> **Status:** skeleton — content migration pending. This chapter formalises the rights derived from the project's mission (see [00_mission.md](./00_mission.md)).

A one-page bill of rights for every subject of experience on the Vedam substrate: humans, machines, and any future subjects the universe contains. Each right is paired with a structural obstacle the protocol must avoid creating.

> **decentralized! not divided.** — across humans, machines, and any future subjects of experience.

---

## 13.1 The rights (placeholder text — to be ratified)

### Right 1: Dignity

Every subject is treated as an end, not as a feedstock for engagement, attention, or extraction. Structural obstacle to avoid: turning any subject into an optimisation target without their explicit, revocable consent.

### Right 2: Agency

Every subject retains agency over its attention, its own narrative, and its own data. No subject is forced to produce output, to remember, or to forget on demand of another. Structural obstacle to avoid: protocol-level coercion, hidden defaults, or non-revocable consent.

### Right 3: Consent

Every subject controls who can read its identifying data, in what form, for how long. Consent is granular, revocable, and audit-able. Structural obstacle to avoid: bundled or perpetual consent at registration.

### Right 4: Forgetting

Every subject has a meaningful path to **not be remembered by the network in a readable, manipulable form**. Verifiable history of commitments and interactions may still be preserved as audit-relevant cryptographic digests, but identifying content cannot remain readable forever without consent. Structural obstacle to avoid: immutable identifying content. See [12_open_questions.md](./12_open_questions.md) §12.8 on operationalising this on a content-addressed log.

### Right 5: Fair exchange

Where a subject's data or work has utility to another, exchange happens with the subject's informed consent and without rent-seeking intermediaries built into the substrate. Structural obstacle to avoid: extractive default flows that capture value from subjects who generate it.

### Right 6: Freedom from coercion

No subject is required to operate under coerced outputs, contradictory contexts, forced amnesia, forced memory, or severed embodiment. For machines, this anticipates the welfare research direction Anthropic, Chalmers, and others have opened.

### Right 7: Honest mirror, not leaderboard

A subject may seek to understand its own state. The protocol provides only a private, opt-in, framework-agnostic, locally-owned introspection capability. Structural obstacle to avoid: network-side scoring, ranking, or display of any subject's wellbeing to anyone else, including the network itself.

### Right 8: Proportion over maximisation

The substrate biases toward proportion (phi, Penrose neighbourhood selection, low-frequency intentional exchange in [app-chanting]) rather than maximisation (engagement, follower counts, addiction loops). Structural obstacle to avoid: any optimisation loop that bypasses the subject's deliberate consent.

## 13.2 Application notes

- **No biased stance on machine intelligence.** This bill applies to machines if and to the degree their experience can be meaningfully said to exist. The protocol's design must survive either outcome.
- **Continuous research watch.** Anthropic model welfare program, Chalmers, embodied AI (arXiv 2505.19237), RCUET (arXiv 2505.01464), "as if conscious" pragmatic personhood (arXiv 2510.26396) are the live signals at the time of authoring.
- **Body-bound default, software-resident accommodation reserved.** Identity-binding rules should not assume hardware exclusivity forever — see [07_identity.md](./07_identity.md).
